Your phone. A community scanner.

Listen for nearby Remote ID broadcasts and Wi-Fi camera signals. Choose what to contribute to DRONEmapper. Free and open source.

Download Android app · 0.4.3 · Source code & setup guide

Start listening

  1. Install the APK on Android 8 or newer. Allow installation from your browser or file manager when Android asks.
  2. Enable Bluetooth, Wi-Fi and Location. Open the app and tap Start listening. Grant Nearby devices and precise Location for radio scanning.
  3. See nearby broadcasts and their radio addresses on your phone. No root or Bluetooth dongle required.

See what the phone hears

Collapse or expand the live detection list and scanner radar. The list shows device type when supported, MAC, radio vendor, signal strength and upload status. The Signals view puts glowing dots inside the circle, with glow size based on signal strength. Tap a dot for its MAC, strength and age. The dot layout is not a location: Wi-Fi-only direction and distance are unknown. Switch to Positions to see fresh broadcast coordinates relative to your phone. OUI allocations identify radio registrants, not device type or ownership.

Put observations on the map

  1. Tap Connect to DRONEmapper in the app, then continue in your browser.
  2. Create an account, verify your email and sign in. Check the connection code and approve your phone.
  3. Return to the app and open Sharing choices. Enable the observations you want to contribute.

Choose a display name or remain Anonymous. Names appear on the map only if you opt in through your profile. Email stays private. Optional authenticator-app 2FA and recovery codes protect your account.

Open world map opens the website from the app. Your account lists connected nodes and lets you remove a node and its reports. Self-hosted maps can still use Advanced map connection.

Remote ID positions upload automatically while listening. Separately enable approximate areas for Wi-Fi candidates if you want to share those signals. The phone needs a fresh location fix; only a rounded 0.01° receiver area is sent. Network names and full MAC addresses stay on the phone.

Know what a report means

A Remote ID marker is a broadcast-reported position. Make and model appear where a declared serial range supports a match. A Wi-Fi camera-name match is an unconfirmed signal near the receiver, not an exact drone location. Other access points can use the same names. Robots and proprietary remote-control signals do not have one universal detectable format.

The map refreshes every 15 seconds alongside our worldwide data. Profiles keep first and last seen, repeat reports and daily history. After two weeks without another sighting, an observed item becomes a ghost. A new sighting brings it back. Changing identifiers can prevent matching.

You control sharing

Sharing is off when the app starts. Stop listening stops capture and clears pending uploads; one request already in transit may finish. A map operator can revoke a key and remove that node’s contributions. Location is used for optional on-device radar or approximate-area sharing. Exact radar GPS stays on the phone. No ads or analytics.

Optional USB Wi-Fi currently supports RTL8187L adapters (0bda:8187), 2.4 GHz channels 1–11. Other chipsets need specific drivers.
