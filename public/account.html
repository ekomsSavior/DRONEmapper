YOUR LOGS. YOUR CHOICE.

Bring data in. Take your data with you.

01 / IMPORT

Already have wardrive logs?

  1. Sign in above and open Wardrive logs & upload tokens.
  2. Choose My signed-in account, select your WiGLE-format CSV and check its timezone. No token is needed for manual uploads.
  3. Preview the matches, then upload to the map.

Import observations from the past year, in files up to 256 MiB. Supported WiFi CAM and Tello-style name matches appear as Possible signals near the receiver. Ordinary Wi-Fi and Bluetooth rows are skipped; this CSV importer does not decode Remote ID.

Original dates are kept. Sightings older than 14 days appear as Ghosts. These are approximate signal areas, not confirmed drone positions.

02 / RECORD OFFLINE

Drive now. Upload at home.

  1. In Android 0.7.0 or later, tap Start listening → Saved wardrives → Record a wardrive.
  2. Keep Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and Location enabled. No internet or account is needed to record.
  3. Finish recording or stop listening to save the trip.
  4. At home, connect the app to your account, open the trip and select Upload later to DRONEmapper.

Trips survive stopping and app restarts. Recording pauses live sharing. Saved uploads are manual; reconnecting to Wi-Fi alone does not send them. Keep the app open during upload. Retrying with the same connection skips duplicates.

03 / EXPORT

Use your logs on another map.

  1. Open a finished trip in Saved wardrives.
  2. Choose Export WiGLE CSV for matching Wi-Fi observations with a fresh GPS fix.
  3. Save the file, then import it into a service that accepts that format using its own account.

The CSV includes Wi-Fi names, MAC addresses and receiver GPS. DRONEmapper JSON is the separate export for native map reports, including decoded Remote ID.

Direct sending to WiGLE and Biscuit is not integrated. WiGLE server acceptance and Biscuit CSV import still need verification. A DRONEmapper token only authorizes uploads to DRONEmapper.

Formats & connection guide ↗

For a trip recorded in our app, use its built-in upload to DRONEmapper. Use CSV exports for other services; importing the same trip here under a second token can create separate reports.

CHOOSE YOUR SETUP

Use the device you have.

Android phone

Android 8 or newer, with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. Start with the radios already in your phone; no root or extra Bluetooth dongle is needed. Reception depends on the phone and the broadcasts nearby.

Live workflow: install → start listening → connect your account → choose what to share. For offline trips, use Saved wardrives instead.

Android download & instructions ↗

Optional USB receivers

Android USB Wi-Fi drivers are included for MT7610U (Panda PAU0D), MT7612U (Alfa AWUS036ACM) and RTL8187L. Compatibility follows the chipset; phone and adapter combinations still need testing.

Use USB OTG or a powered hub → select the receiver in the app → Connect → Allow. Android HackRF support provides RF reception and a spectrum display; RF peaks alone do not identify drones.

USB Wi-Fi & HackRF setup ↗

Windows, macOS or Linux

Run Desktop node 0.6.4 with Python 3.10+ and compatible Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, HackRF, RTL-SDR or ESP32/CYD serial receivers. The same radar and account connection are available on all three systems.

Workflow: download and extract → run your system’s launcher → set up receivers → connect your account → choose sharing → start listening.

Radio reception depends on OS drivers and hardware. Native Windows/macOS reception still needs field testing.

Desktop download & setup ↗

You can import an existing CSV from a computer or phone browser without any scanner hardware. Live browsing of the world map is also free and needs no receiver.

Robot signal contributions

Android 0.8.5 and Desktop 0.6.4 recognize a supported CyberDog Bluetooth pattern as a possible robot dog. In your node, separately enable Share approximate robot signal areas to contribute a rounded receiver area. Saved Android wardrives with a fresh location fix can upload these reports later or export DRONEmapper JSON. WiGLE CSV remains Wi-Fi-only.
